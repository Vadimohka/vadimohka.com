01 · Decisions

The decisions an enterprise AI initiative has to survive.

Use-case selection

Choose a workflow where data, ownership, review and value can be made explicit.

Architecture

Decide how models, retrieval, integrations, access and deployment boundaries fit together.

Governance

Define evaluation, human review, source grounding, audit and escalation before rollout.

Operating ownership

Make product, security, engineering and risk responsibilities visible after the pilot.

02 · Operating path

From a priority decision to a controlled production boundary.

01

Frame

Clarify the decision, users, constraints, data and success conditions.

02

Design

Map product scope, architecture, access boundaries, evaluation and review.

03

Test

Use source-grounded scenarios and operational checks to expose risk early.

04

Operate

Set ownership, observability, escalation and deployment controls for the next boundary.

This is a method for making a decision and its constraints explicit; it is not a guarantee of delivery or an implied client case.

03 · Governance and security context

Controls belong in the product and operating model.

The relevant questions are practical: who can access which data, how sources are grounded, when a human reviews an output, what is logged, what is observable, and which deployment model is acceptable.

  • Access boundaries and data handling
  • Source grounding and evaluation
  • Human review and escalation
  • Audit and observability
  • Private, on-premise or VPC deployment choices

04 · Deliverables and boundaries

A useful engagement leaves decisions, owners and next checks behind.

Decision brief

Priority use case, constraints, stakeholders, assumptions and the decision to be made.

Operating design

Product and architecture boundary, access model, evaluation plan and human-review path.

Production readiness view

Open risks, ownership questions, evidence still needed and a scoped next step.

The work does not include a security certification, a promise of regulatory approval, or a named client outcome without a confirmed source.
