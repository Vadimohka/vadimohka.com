When product, architecture and engineering diverge.

01 · Trigger conditions

You may need a diagnostic when…

Roadmap and architecture diverge

Product commitments depend on technical decisions nobody has made explicit.

Busy does not mean predictable

The team is active, but delivery, ownership and review remain unclear.

Pilot work cannot become a product

AI scope is larger than the evidence, workflow or operating model can support.

Commercial commitments are blocked

Engineering risks, integration questions or unclear ownership make the next promise unsafe.

Decisions keep reopening

Architecture, product and team trade-offs are revisited without a shared decision map.

The next boundary is unclear

There is no agreed sequence for what to fix, test, defer or own next.

02 · Diagnostic process

Start with the decision, then inspect the system around it.

  1. Clarify the product decision and operating constraint
  2. Review product scope, architecture, information and ownership
  3. Prioritise risks by consequence and reversibility
  4. Agree the next cadence, decision owners and checks

05 · Boundaries

A diagnostic is not fundraising or a growth promise.

The work focuses on product, architecture, engineering ownership and delivery decisions. It does not include fundraising advice, introductions, investment advice, exit planning or a promise of growth. Testimonials and commercial outcomes are shared only when separately approved and evidenced.
