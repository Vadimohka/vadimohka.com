# Claim safety policy

## Claim levels

### Level A — independently verified

A primary or authoritative source proves the person, role or event, date, and material wording.

Allowed in:

- hero proof;
- Track Record;
- metadata;
- JSON-LD;
- media bio;
- AI-readable profile files.

### Level B — owner-confirmed and narrowly scoped

The owner can truthfully verify the claim, but the work is confidential or has no public source.

Allowed only when:

- wording is specific;
- scope is personal;
- no client identity is revealed without permission;
- a date/range exists;
- the claim does not imply independent validation;
- a safe anonymised context is provided when useful.

Not suitable as the only hero proof.

### Level C — partially verified

A source supports only part of a claim.

Allowed only in the narrower form actually supported.

Example:

- verified: a Stepik course exists;
- not yet verified: 4,700+ current learners.

### Level D — company-level only

The source proves something about StackLevel or Century, not Vadim's personal contribution.

Allowed on a product/company context page with explicit attribution. It may not be converted into “Vadim delivered X” without personal mandate evidence.

### Level E — unsupported or reputationally risky

Omit from production.

## Numbers

A number requires:

- definition;
- period;
- unit;
- source;
- last-checked date;
- owner permission.

Never round a vague recollection into a polished metric.

## Client and sector claims

A sector label is not harmless when the audience is institutional. “Banks” implies domain and risk experience.

Use one of:

1. named case with permission;
2. anonymised case with sufficient context and personal mandate;
3. “focus” or “designed for” wording;
4. omit.

Do not use a company logo strip as personal proof.

## Roles

Use exact titles. Distinguish:

- current vs former;
- company vs product;
- legal entity vs initiative;
- founder vs co-founder;
- CTO vs Head of R&D;
- speaker vs panel participant;
- author vs quoted source.

## Education

Use institution, programme/degree, dates, and verification. A vague “MIPT signal” is not acceptable.

## Media

A public signal card requires:

- title;
- outlet/event;
- date;
- exact role;
- working URL;
- optional recording;
- no hidden TODO.

## Confidential work

NDA is not a reason to publish vague grandeur. A safe anonymised record can still state:

- organisation type;
- decision;
- personal mandate;
- team range;
- technical scope;
- constraints;
- non-sensitive outcome;
- what cannot be disclosed.

## Machine-readable parity

JSON-LD and AI profile files must be at least as conservative as visible copy. Search/AI files are not a place to hide broader claims.

## Removal rule

When in doubt, remove or narrow the claim. A smaller verified site is stronger than a larger unverifiable one.
